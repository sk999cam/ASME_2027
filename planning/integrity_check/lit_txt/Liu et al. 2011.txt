a b s t r a c t
This paper presents the new development on the turbulent Prandtl number distribution models which
are used to improve the computation accuracy of ﬁlm cooling effectiveness distribution in the frame
of the isotropic turbulence models. In the new developed turbulent Prandtl number distribution models,
the turbulent Prandtl number varies with some characteristic quantity of the ﬁlm cooling to make the
models applicable for more ﬁlm cooling cases effectively. A new VTG-Prt model is developed for the computation of ﬁlm cooling cases in which the wall is mainly inﬂuenced by the mixing layer region between
the jet core and the mainstream. The Prt value in this model varies with dimensionless temperature gradient which is suggested to be the characteristic quantity of the mixing layer region. The VTG-Prt model
has been used in the computations of long cylindrical hole ﬁlm cooling, short cylindrical hole ﬁlm cooling,
compound angle cylindrical hole ﬁlm cooling and expansion shaped hole ﬁlm cooling in the present
paper. And it is validated to be an effective and easy way to obtain accurate ﬁlm cooling effectiveness
distributions for this kind of ﬁlm cooling. But for the kind of ﬁlm cooling, in which the wall is mainly
inﬂuenced by the jet core region and the inﬂuence of heat diffusion in the mixing layer region on the cooling effectiveness is very small, the VTG-Prt model is disabled. Another turbulent Prandtl number distribution model, named VT-Prt model, is developed for this kind of ﬁlm cooling in the present paper. The
Prt value in this model varies with dimensionless temperature which is suggested to be the characteristic
quantity of the jet core region. Good agreement between the computed and measured results is obtained
through the use of VT-Prt model in the computation of converging slot hole ﬁlm cooling.

1. Introduction
The desire for higher overall efﬁciency and higher power output
in gas turbines render the need for increase in turbine entry temperatures. This places severe demands on the components working
in high temperature environment, such as turbine rotors and
vanes. The turbine blades cannot withstand these temperatures.
Consequently, cooling of gas turbine components is inevitable,
and ﬁlm cooling is widely used as an effective means to maintain
component temperatures at acceptable levels.
Nowadays numerical simulation on ﬁlm cooling investigation is
getting more and more important, because of its low-cost,
labor-saved and complete-data characteristics. However, turbulence models employed in ﬁlm cooling calculations in general do
not go beyond the isotropic two-equation scope, while turbulence in ﬁlm-cooling ﬂows is considerably anisotropic [1]. Hoda
and Acharya [2] explored seven representative two-equation

Prandtl number Prt is an important parameter for the prediction of
cooling effectiveness because its value affects the accuracy of heat
diffusion directly. Kays [13] suggested a value of 0.85 for Prt which
is the same as the Prt value in the boundary layer ‘‘log’’ region. This
value is often employed in the ﬁlm cooling computation. But the
ﬁlm cooling ﬂow ﬁeld includes not only the boundary layer, but
also the mixing layer (or the shear layer). One major difference between these regions and the inner core of boundary layer is the value of Prt. According to the studies of Mayer and Divoky’s [14],
Wygnanski and Fiedler [15], Fiedler [16], Townsend [17], Chambers and Antonia [18], Chidambaram et al. [19] and Henze [20],
the value of Prt in the shear layer is in the range of 0.3–0.8. For ﬁlm
cooling ﬂow ﬁeld, Kohli and Bogard [21] reported some information of Prt in the mixing layer region and the jet region. Although
the information was not very plenty, it still showed that the value
of Prt was different from the usually used value around 0.9.
On the base of Azzi et al. [6], Lakehal [22] considered the Prt
distribution in the inner core of boundary layer and made the computed effectiveness spanwise distribution more close to the
measurement. Good agreement with the experimental data was
obtained by using the turbulent Prandtl number correction in the
four-equation turbulence model in Jones et al. [4]. Liu et al. [23]
extended the idea of Lakehal [22] and paid attention to the value
of Prt in the mixing layer region of ﬁlm cooling. In the frame of
the isotropic two-equation turbulence model, Liu et al. [23] studied
the effect of turbulent Prandtl number in the ﬂow ﬁeld outside the
near-wall region on the computational results of cylindrical hole’s
cooling effectiveness distribution. The results show that the variation of turbulent Prandtl number had great inﬂuence on the computational results. Liu et al. [23] also introduced a turbulent
Prandtl number distribution model named laterally varying turbulent Prandtl number (LV-Prt) model in which the turbulent Prandtl
number is dependent on lateral location and blowing ratio. Computation accuracy is improved by this LV-Prt model when using
isotropic two-equation turbulence model.

2. Computational approach
According to the analysis in Liu et al. [23], the LV-Prt model
introduced in that paper is effective in the computation of simple
angle cylindrical hole ﬁlm cooling. But that turbulent Prandtl number distribution model with respect to lateral location and blowing
ratio has some inherent drawbacks. Firstly, the LV-Prt model only
includes the spanwise distribution of the turbulent Prandtl
number. The variations of the turbulent Prandtl number in the
streamwise direction and the normal direction are not taken into
account. Secondly, in the LV-Prt model, the distribution of the turbulent Prandtl number is symmetrical to the z/D = 0 plane which is
the lateral center plane of the ﬁlm hole. When the ﬁlm hole is only
streamwise oriented and the ﬂow ﬁeld and the temperature ﬁeld
are symmetrical to the lateral center plane of the ﬁlm hole, the
LV-Prt model is workable. In the case that the ﬂow/temperature
ﬁeld of ﬁlm cooling is not symmetrical, such as the ﬁlm cooling
of hole with compound angle orientation, the LV-Prt model cannot
be applied. Thirdly, in the LV-Prt model, the turbulent Prandtl number value is dependent on the lateral position which is not the
characteristic quantity of the ﬁlm cooling. The above three reasons
restrict the application of the LV-Prt model. To further accomplish
the idea of assigning proper value or distribution of Prt to the ﬁlm
cooling ﬂow ﬁeld introduced by Liu et al. [23], two new turbulent

2.4. Computational method
The governing equations are solved by a three-dimensional
ﬁnite-volume method for arbitrary nonorthogonal grids. The discretization scheme for all the transport equations’ convection
terms is QUICK scheme which is of third order precision. The diffusive ﬂuxes are approximated using the central-difference scheme.

The velocity–pressure coupling is achieved by using the wellknown SIMPLEC algorithm. All the difference equations are solved
sequentially with the implicit procedure algorithm. And the convergence in all cases is determined based on a drop in normalized
mass and momentum residuals by four orders of magnitude (104)
at least and the mass-weighted average temperature in the crosssection at the streamwise position x/D = 10 changes less than 0.1%
with increasing iterations. The algebraic multi-grid method is
employed to speed up the converging process.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. VTG-Prt model
As introduced in Section 1, the LV-Prt model has some inherent
drawbacks which restrict the application of the model. In this section a new turbulent Prandtl number distribution model named
VTG-Prt model which can overcome the problems of the LV-Prt
model is introduced based on the idea that identifying the important region by some characteristic quantity of the ﬁlm cooling ﬁeld.

measurement technique. The VTG-Prt model is also applied in the

4. Conclusion
The aim of the work in the present paper is to further accomplish the idea to improve the computation accuracy of ﬁlm cooling
effectiveness in the frame of the isotropic turbulence models by
developing turbulent Prandtl number distribution models. To
make the turbulent Prandtl number distribution models general
and applicable for more different ﬁlm cooling cases effectively,
the turbulent Prandtl number should be dependent on some characteristic quantity of the ﬁlm cooling.
For the kind of ﬁlm cooling, in which the wall is mainly inﬂuenced by the mixing layer region between the jet and the mainstream, the heat diffusion in the mixing layer region has great
inﬂuence on the cooling effectiveness distribution and a new
VTG-Prt model is developed for the cooling effectiveness computation of such ﬁlm cooling cases. The Prt value in this model varies
with dimensionless temperature gradient which is suggested and
used in the present paper to identify the mixing layer region and
to be the characteristic quantity of the mixing layer region for this
turbulent Prandtl number distribution model. Through the applications of the VTG-Prt model in the long cylindrical hole ﬁlm cooling,
short cylindrical hole ﬁlm cooling, compound angle cylindrical hole
ﬁlm cooling and expansion shaped hole ﬁlm cooling, the VTG-Prt
model is validated to be an effective and easy way to improve the
computation accuracy of ﬁlm cooling effectiveness distribution in
the frame of isotropic turbulence model for this kind of ﬁlm cooling.
But the VTG-Prt model is disabled for such kind of ﬁlm cooling,
in which the ﬁlm is very thin and attaches on the wall very well,
and the heat diffusion in the jet core region plays major inﬂuence
on the cooling effectiveness distribution, such as the console hole
ﬁlm cooling. For such ﬁlm cooling case, the dimensionless temperature is suggested to be the characteristic quantity of the jet core
region for this kind of ﬁlm cooling to build new turbulent Prandtl
number distribution model: VT-Prt model. In this model, the value
of Prt varies with dimensionless temperature. Good agreement
between the computational results and experimental results is
obtained through the use of VT-Prt model in the test case of console
hole ﬁlm cooling.
