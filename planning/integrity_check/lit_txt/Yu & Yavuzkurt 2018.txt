ABSTRACT
Modeling the heat transfer characteristics of the highly
turbulent flow in gas turbine film cooling is important for better
engineering solutions to the film cooling system design.
URANS, LES, DES and modified DES models capability in
simulating film cooling with a density ratio of 2.0 and blowing
ratio of 1.0 are studied in this work. Detailed comparisons of
simulation results with experimental data regarding the nearfield and far-fields are made. For near field predictions, DES
gives decent prediction with a 21.4 % deviation of centerline
effectiveness, while LES and URANS have deviation of 33.6%
and 51.2% compared to the experimental data. Despite good
predictions for near field, DES under predicts the spanwise
spreading of counter rotating vortex pair and temperature field,
therefore it over predicts the centerline effectiveness in the far
field. To compensate for this shortcoming of DES, the eddy
viscosity in the spanwise direction is increased to enhance
spanwise-diffusion of the cooling jets. The modified DES
prediction of overall centerline effectiveness deviates 12.4%
from experimental data, while LES, unmodified DES and
URANS predictions deviate 10.8%, 31.9% and 46.9%. The
modified DES model has adequate predictions of vortices
evolutions which URANS modeling lacks and consumes
significant less computational time than LES. It can be said that
the modified DES model results in satisfactory film cooling
modeling with a moderate computational cost and time.

INTRODUCTION
Gas turbine thermal efficiency can be improved by allowing
higher turbine inlet temperature. In today’s gas turbine industry,
the gas turbine blade inlet temperature can be as high as 1600 oC
[1, 2], which is much higher than the melting temperature of
turbine blade material. Thus, delicate internal cooling [3] and
film cooling [4] designs are used to cool the turbine blade and
protect the turbine blade from damage. For the film cooling, a
secondary cold air is injected from multiple holes on the surface
of turbine blade and forms a protective film over the turbine
blade. The complexity of film cooling flow comes from its
highly turbulent nature. Better understanding of the turbulent
fluid flow and turbulent heat transfer mechanisms in film cooling
is critical for improving the turbine blade cooling performance.
Review of experimental studies on film cooling can be found in
open literature [5]. In a film cooling experiment, the flat plate
surface temperature behind inclined cooling hole could be
measured with thermocouples [6, 7, 8], infrared photography [9,
10], or pressure sensitive paint (PSP) [11, 12]. The turbulent flow
structures are analyzed based on temperature measurements, and
a direct study of the jet and cross-flow interactions is lacking.
Although in recent experimental studies, particle image
velocimetry (PIV) systems [13, 14] are used to visualize internal
flow structures, however, the cost and availability of PIV
systems pose limitations. Computational fluid dynamics (CFD)
has provided a possibility. With appropriate CFD simulations,
the fluid flow features and mechanisms in film cooling flow can
be elucidated. At the same time, CFD simulation can
significantly reduce effort, time and cost in gas turbine film
cooling development, and benefit the gas turbine industry.
Extensive research has been performed on film cooling
modeling using Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS)
equations. In a study of Hassan and Yavuzkurt [15], four
turbulence models: the standard k-ε, RNG, realizable k- ε model,
and the standard k-ω model under low free stream turbulence
(FST) intensity were investigated and compared. In a following
study [16], those models performance were further studied under
high FST. It was found under high FST with high blowing ratio
(M=1.5), all the models fail to give good predictions of the
centerline effectiveness. In general, RANS model was found to
underpredict the spanwise spreading and mixing of the cooling
jet [17]. To tackle the issue of spanwise spreading
underprediction in RANS, Lakehal et al. [18] adopted an
anisotropy correction of the eddy viscosity proposed by Bergeles
et al. [19]. The correction multiplies a factor to the eddy viscosity
appearing in the spanwise components of the Reynolds stresses
and heat fluxes. With this modification, the RANS model better
predicts spanwise temperature spreading and then centerline
effectiveness [20]. In following studies of Li et al. [21, 22], this
anisotropic eddy viscosity method was validated by comparing
with various sets of experimental data [21] and then using a large
scale turbine blade cooling RANS simulation [22].
Large eddy simulation (LES) has been used to study the
turbulence structure and development of vortices in film cooling.
In an early study, Tyagi and Acharya [23] performed LES to
investigate the complex flow field created by jet and cross-flow

interactions. Detailed flow structures like counter rotating vortex
pair (CRVP), roller vortices, and upright wake vortices were
found in their LES modeling, and the hair-pin shaped vortices in
the coolant jet were analyzed in their study. Guo et al. [24]
conducted LES study on film cooling and focused on the
development of CRVP. The perpendicular injection and inclined
injection were both studied, and their effects on CRVP formation
and evolution were discussed. Peet and Lele [25] studied near
field of film cooling jet with LES. Three vortex systems were
described in their study to characterize the interactions of jet and
cross-flow: horse shoe vortex, downstream spiral separation
node and CRVP. Comparison of their LES modeling with
experimentally measured mean velocity and turbulence statistics
were in good agreement. Fujimoto [26] investigated octree
hexahedral meshes density effect on the prediction accuracy of
film cooling and pointed out that for blowing ratio of 0.5 the
mesh dependency is weak while highly dense mesh (6.2 million)
is needed for blowing ratio of 1.0 regarding film cooling.
Kalghatgi and Acharya [27] performed LES on near field
modeling of film cooling problem under mass ratio of 2.0 and
blowing ratio of 1.0 with a mesh of 8 million, and had good
prediction compared with experimental data. Oliver et al. [28]
provided high fidelity LES simulation at near field region of a
shaped hole under density ratio of 1.6, blowing ratio of 2.0 with
a mesh of 15.9 million, and good match of the centerline and
spanwise effectiveness, and the temperature field had been
achieved.
An alternative modeling method is detached-eddy
simulation (DES). DES is a hybrid of RANS and LES models
aiming to resolve high Reynolds number separated flows. Early
application of DES was on CFD simulation on air foil flow [29].
The first DES of film cooling was presented by Roy et al. [30],
and they found the DES modeling captured more dynamic flow
structures than RANS. Kim and Hassan [31] conducted a
comparison study of DES with unsteady RANS (URANS) and
LES. DES modeling successfully predicts the evolution of the
typical vortical structures in jet cross-flow interaction and gives
adequate modeling results when compared with experimental
data. In Foroutan and Yavuzkurt [32]’s study, DES modeling was
compared with RANS and experimental results. It was found
DES resulted in better centerline effectiveness than RANS, a
good match of experimental results under blowing ratio of 0.5
and reasonable match under blowing ratio of 1.5. A new version
DES, delayed DES was introduced to better handle transition
from RANS to LES model [33]. In a review by Spalart [34], the
strength and weakness of DES method was discussed,
applications of DES in various applications, majorly in
aerospace engineering, were also summarized. Although DES
has been an available approach for years, DES modeling on film
cooling is still at its early stage. As found in the present study,
the delayed DES costs significantly shorten the computational
time compared to LES on exactly the same mesh set, which poses
new meaning of DES. In a recent study [35], measurements of
temperature distribution behind a row of holes at different
blowing ratios was performed. It was found under the large
blowing ratio, the flow behind nearby rows interacts with each

For film cooling modeling, the near field behavior is critical
for several reasons. First, in today’s gas turbine film cooling
design, the cooling holes are quite near with each other. For the
far field, actually the flow behind each hole can interact with
each other. So, for the realistic case, a long far field region may
not exist. Although it is meaningful for modeling and exploring
both near field and far field modeling based on available
experiment data [5-14], the near field modeling depicts the
temperature near the cooling hole. Secondly, at the near field, the
jet and cross-flow have strong interactions. The strong shear
generates large eddies and various vortex structures. Thus,
resolving the near field problem is challenging but important
one.
The goal of this study is to draw attention and compare
different turbulence models in predicting the near-field of film
cooling. In addition, this study gives specific analysis of the
spanwise temperature field and discusses prediction of the
spanwise temperature field spreading for different models. At
last, this study points out that the z-diffusion is important in film
cooing modeling. An anisotropic eddy viscosity modification is
made in the DES model. With this modification, DES model
better predicts the spanwise temperature field spread as well as
the centerline effectiveness. With exactly same mesh, this DES
model has better prediction in the near field region while costing
about one fourth computational time compared with LES
modeling. The computational domain is set to be equivalent to
the experimental study by Sinha et al. [40], in which film cooling
with 7 inclined cylindrical film cooling holes in one row on a flat
plate is studied. It is worthwhile to mention that only one
working condition with density ratio of 2.0, blowing ratio of 1.0
is explored in detail and thoroughly compared with well
documented experimental data available. For a typical gas
turbine engine condition, density ratio is around 2.0 [42]. And
for the mass ratio of 1.0, the lift off and interactions between the
mainstream and coolant jet are strong. Thus, density ratio of 2.0
and mass ratio of 1.0 is usually chosen for a representative
condition to investigate turbulence interactions of mainstream
and coolant jet. Also this condition has been chosen as a typical
condition for evaluating and comparing different models on gas
turbine cooling modeling in several previous studies [26, 27].
The mesh is generated with ANSYS ICEM and modeling is
performed with ANSYS Fluent [41] with UDF development.
Studies of near field simulation of cooling for lower density and
blowing ratios is continuing. In recent years, more and more
experimental data which resolves temperature contours using

techniques like PSP and IR imaging is available [11, 12].
Comparison of modeling results with experimental measured
contours will further help to prove the validity of these models
in predicting gas turbine blade film cooling using CFD. Ongoing
research by the authors is also on comparing the predicted
temperature contours with experimental data.

Geometry, Mesh and Time Step
The test case is set up based on the experimental study by
Sinha et al. [40] as shown in Figure 1. In their experiment, a row
of 7 inclined cylindrical film cooling holes injected cool jet at a
temperature of 150 K into the hot freestream at temperature of
300K over a flat plate. The diameter of film cooling holes is
0.0127m. Centerline and spanwise distribution of film cooling
effectiveness were measured. The jet and mainstream
corresponding to one injection hole is chosen as the modeling
domain. In regards to using a single hole and corresponding
domain to represent the experiment with a row of holes, if
simulated jet spreads and reaches z/d = 1.5, i.e. the mid-plane
between two film cooling holes, then it would be necessary to
include more holes in the case to simulate the interaction
between two jets, and possibly higher levels of advection and
diffusion can be predicted. According to the experimental data,
at least at x/D = 15, jet spread already reaches z/D = 1.5. But for
URANS, LES and DES since they fail to predict enough
spanwise spreading, and the jets do not interact with each other
at x/D=15. Therefore, a single hole or a row of holes simulation
is expected to not to make any difference except the latter will
take more time and storage. For DES with 4 z-diffusion with
higher rate of jet spread, dimensionless temperature at x/D = 15,
z/D = 1.5 is 0.051, which might increase and move closer to
Sinha et al.’s [40] data, if a row of holes is included to the
simulation.

emerging experimental results on the temperature field in gas
turbine film cooling obtained using infrared photography or PSP
techniques becomes widely available for comparisons.

Time averaging of unsteady modeling results is made in the
following manner. One in every five time steps is chosen for time
averaging, and 3000 data samples are averaged. Thus, temporal
results over period of 15000 time steps are averaged for
exploring and comparing the characteristics of results obtained
using different models. As shown in Figure 7, the spanwise
mixing between the jet and the mainstream is very low in
URANS model results. This underprediction of spanwise mixing
causes insufficient expansion of the dimensionless temperature
contour. This explains why in URANS modeling, the
temperature contour is very narrow near the wall resulting in
high centerline effectiveness. Contours of dimensionless
temperature for the DES and anisotropic eddy viscosity modified
DES, and LES are overall wider. Because of more spanwise
mixing, the part of the cooling jet that touches the wall is warmer
in those models than the URANS model. Therefore those models
tend to give lower film cooling effectiveness than URANS and
are closer to the experiment.

x, y, and z directions, they have different diffusion behavior in
different directions indeed. The diffusion is found to be
anisotropic by nature, thus assuming isotropic diffusion may
cause problems. In the experimental study from Quarmby and
Quirk [38], they showed that eddy viscosity is larger in lateral
direction compared to the normal direction both for momentum
and energy transport. Thus, it was decided that by applying an
anisotropic viscosity and enhance eddy viscosity in z direction,
the spanwise spreading of the temperature field will be enhanced.
And this is the motivation in studying 4 z-diffusion modified
DES. The underlying mechanisms here needs further exploration
in the future.

CONCLUSIONS
In this paper, different modeling methods of URANS, LES,
DES and DES with enhanced z-diffusion modification are
applied to simulate the film cooling performance of a single
inline inclined hole flow simulations with density ratio of 2.0 and
blowing ratio of 1.0. Different Predictions obtained using
different models particularly in the near-field of film cooling jet
are compared in detail. Based on the centerline effectiveness, it
is found that URANS, DES, modified DES, and LES result in
54.2%, 21.4%, 27.9% and 33.6% deviations from experimental
data respectively. For an overall comparison including both near
field and far field, the URANS, DES, modified DES and LES
predictions deviate 46.9%, 31.9%, 12.4% and 10.8% from
experimental data. In URANS modeling results, the jet retains its
identity for a long distance downstream, and the mixing between
the jet and mainstream is very low. LES does not give best results
in the near field, on the other hand, DES gives good predictions
in the near field of film cooling. LES captures finest vortical
structures while DES also results in prediction of the vortical
structures and eddies evolution to a lesser extend. In the far field
for x/D > 10, DES does not give good results and the
effectiveness stays high. To improve DES modeling, anisotropic
eddy viscosity correction is adopted in DES modeling, and 4 zdiffusion modification is recommended. Although this
anisotropic eddy viscosity correction has been proposed for
improving RANS modeling in film cooling in earlier studies, it
is the first time this method is applied to DES modeling to the
authors’ knowledge. This enhanced z-diffusion DES model
captures typical vortical structures and most importantly
enhances spanwise spreading of CRVP in film cooling. Beyond
the centerline effectiveness, the 4 z-diffusion DES is found to
better match the local spanwise distribution of effectiveness and
spanwise-averaged effectiveness at different x/D locations. The
advantage of DES model is that it resolves enough details of
vortical structures and turbulent eddies and at the same time, is
computationally efficient. This is very important when extending
the film cooling modeling work for more realistic schemes with
