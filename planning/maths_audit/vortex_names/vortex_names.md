# Established names for mean streamwise vortices beside the CRVP (film cooling, jet in crossflow)

Status: literature check complete to the extent the sources were accessible. Date of check: 2026-11-27.
Method: web search plus page fetches. "VERIFIED" = text read in a fetched page, abstract, or extracted PDF.
"UNVERIFIED" = taken from a search-result snippet, a search summary, or memory; the page itself was not read.
Sense convention used below: "same" or "opposite" refers to the adjacent kidney vortex (CRVP vortex on the same side).

Structures of interest:
- (1) near-wall vortex immediately outboard of the CRVP vortex, OPPOSITE sign to it
- (2) weaker vortex further outboard, SAME sign as the CRVP vortex
- (3) horseshoe-shaped Q rim around the upstream edge of the hole exit, legs trailing downstream

## Findings on sign convention

- Haven and Kurosaka 1997 define "anti-kidney" by sign: opposite to the kidney vortices. Their kidney (lower-deck) pair has the same sense as the CRVP; the upper-deck pair may have either sense depending on hole geometry.
- "Wall vortices" (Fric and Roshko tradition) is a category defined by origin (wall boundary-layer vorticity). No source read assigns it a sign relative to the CRVP.
- "Horseshoe vortex" is defined by origin and shape (junction flow wrapped around the jet base). The horseshoe legs on the wall carry vorticity of the sign set by the approach boundary layer, which is opposite to the sign of the downwash-side kidney vortex only in some configurations. This was not confirmed from any source and is not asserted.
- No read source names a mean streamwise vortex that is both outboard of the CRVP and of the SAME sign as it (structure 2). The "lower-deck" pair of Haven and Kurosaka has the kidney sense but is the kidney pair itself. The "chimney vortex" (patent wording, UNVERIFIED) is the only candidate with that description.
